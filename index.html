About

Who is Senquiii?

Nobody's sure where the first Senquiii beat came from. It just showed up one night: one loop, no name, no explanation.

The name is spelled with three I's and said “Senkwe.” The music sits somewhere between late-night boom bap, dusty blues, and the kind of melodies you hum without knowing why. Senquiii doesn't do interviews and doesn't explain the songs. The work speaks, and anything it doesn't say is up to you.

What is known: Senquiii started with almost nothing. No studio, no expensive gear, no one opening doors. Just a cheap setup, free software, and a stubborn ear. That's why this lab exists. Everything that took years to figure out is here, free, for whoever's next.

The channel became a home for independent artists who wanted reach without giving up who they are. The lab is the other half: the tools, the lessons, and the beats, so you can build your own sound from day one.

Cheap

$0

Everything you need to finish a song.

Software (DAW)

  • BandLab: free in the browser and on phones. The best place to start on a phone.
  • GarageBand: free on Mac, iPhone, and iPad.
  • Cakewalk Sonar (Windows): free tier, unlimited tracks. Replaces the old Cakewalk by BandLab.
  • LMMS: free and open-source, great for beats.
  • Waveform Free: full DAW, no track limits.
  • Pro Tools Intro · Audacity for vocal editing.

Recording

  • Your phone. Record inside a closet full of clothes, or under a thick blanket. Both kill room echo.
  • Hold the phone 15–30 cm (6–12 in) away and slightly off to the side, so your p's and b's don't pop.
  • Wear earbuds for the beat so it doesn't bleed into the mic.

Sounds & plugins

  • Freesound (filter to CC0) · Looperman · Musopen (public-domain classical) · Free Music Archive
  • Vital synth · Spitfire LABS · Decent Sampler · TDR Nova EQ · Kotelnikov compressor · Valhalla Supermassive reverb
Low

~$60–$200

The best upgrades per dollar.

Software (DAW)

  • REAPER: ~$60 personal license, with a fully working 60-day trial. Runs on old laptops.
  • FL Studio Fruity: ~$99, lifetime free updates. It can't record audio, so get Producer (~$179) for vocals.
  • Ableton Live Intro: ~$99 (16 tracks).
  • Bitwig Essentials: ~$99 · Pro Tools Artist: ~$99/yr.

Gear

  • A USB mic or an entry audio interface + dynamic mic (dynamic mics forgive untreated rooms).
  • Closed-back studio headphones beat cheap speakers in a bedroom.
  • A 25- or 32-key MIDI keyboard if you program melodies.
High

$199+

When music is your main thing.

Software (DAW)

  • Logic Pro (Mac): ~$199.99 one-time. Huge stock library.
  • FL Studio All Plugins: ~$449.
  • Ableton Live Suite: ~$749, or rent-to-own.
  • Pro Tools Studio: ~$299/yr, the studio standard.
  • Cubase Pro · Fender Studio Pro (formerly Studio One).

Gear

  • Studio monitors + basic acoustic treatment (treat the room before buying fancy speakers).
  • A large-diaphragm condenser mic for a treated space.
  • Paid plugin bundles after you know exactly what's missing.

No lyric generator here, on purpose. Your voice is the one thing nobody can download. These are frameworks and prompts to get you moving. The words have to be yours.

The universal shape

  1. Intro4–8 bars
  2. Verse 116 bars rap · 8 sung
  3. Hook / Chorus8 bars
  4. Verse 216 / 8 bars
  5. Hook8 bars
  6. Bridgeoptional, 4–8
  7. Final Hook + Outro

One bar = four beats. Count “1-2-3-4” over the beat. Each number is one beat.

Three frameworks that always work

Show, don't tell
Don't write “I was sad.” Write what sad looked like: the cold coffee, the unread texts. Specific images hit harder than labels.
Object writing
Pick one object in the room. Write for 10 minutes using all five senses. Mine it for lines.
Hook first
Hum melodies over the beat into voice memos before words. Keep the catchiest 2 seconds and build the song around it.

Frameworks by style

Hip-hop / Rap

  • 16-bar verses, 8-bar hook.
  • Stack multisyllable and internal rhymes; vary your flow every 4 bars.
  • Punchline at bar 4, 8, 12, 16. Land it on the snare.
  • Fewer words at slow tempos means more space for cadence.

R&B / Soul

  • Melody first, words second.
  • Open vowels (“oh,” “ay,” “you”) on long notes sing better.
  • Tell a moment, not a whole relationship.
  • Pre-chorus builds tension. Chorus releases it.

Rock / Alt

  • Verse–chorus with a big, shoutable chorus.
  • Short, punchy lines that ride the guitar rhythm.
  • Contrast: a quiet verse makes a loud chorus louder.
  • Let the bridge flip the perspective.

Blues

  • AAB form: line one, repeat it (maybe tweaked), then answer it.
  • Fits the 12-bar progression, 4 bars per line.
  • Leave space after each line for the guitar to “answer.”
  • Real trouble, plain words, a little humor.

Pop

  • Title in the chorus, ideally first or last line.
  • Repeat the hook more than feels comfortable.
  • Simple melody, conversational lyrics.
  • Get to the chorus within ~45 seconds.

Lo-fi / Melodic

  • Short phrases, lots of space, soft delivery.
  • Mood over story: one feeling, many images.
  • Repetition becomes texture.
  • Whisper-close vocals: record near the mic, quietly.

Writing prompt

Write about the last time you almost said something, but didn't.

Picking a tempo

Tempo = BPM (beats per minute). Start in your genre's range, then nudge until your head nods on its own.

GenreBPM
R&B / slow jams60–80
Lo-fi70–90
Boom bap hip-hop85–98
Blues shuffle70–120
House120–128
Trap (half-time feel)130–150
Drill138–145
Rock110–170

Half-time trick: 140 BPM with the snare on beat 3 feels like 70. That's why trap feels slow and fast at once.

Making it sound good

Arrangement
Change something every 4–8 bars: drop the drums, add a layer, filter the beat. Leave space when the vocal enters.
Levels first
Balance faders before any plugin. Keep the master peaking around −6 dB for headroom. Never in the red.
EQ
Cut before you boost. High-pass everything that isn't kick or bass. Muddy? Dip 200–400 Hz. Harsh? Dip 2–5 kHz.
Compression
Vocal starter: 3:1–4:1 ratio, 3–6 dB of gain reduction. If it sounds squashed, back off.
Reverb & delay
Use sends, not inserts. Less than you think.
Reference tracks
Drop in a song you love, match its loudness, and A/B the low end, vocal level, and brightness.
Check every mix on earbuds, a phone speaker, and in a car, because that's how people will actually hear it. Then sleep on it. Fresh ears catch everything.

Push Your Music

Finishing is half. Pushing is the other half.

Great songs don't find people on their own. Here's how to get yours out there without a label.

A

Distribution

  • Use a distributor to reach Spotify, Apple Music, TikTok, and more. Options include DistroKid, TuneCore, CD Baby, Amuse, and RouteNote. Pricing models differ (yearly vs. per release, some free tiers), so compare current terms and keep 100% of your rights.
  • Upload 3–4 weeks early so you can pitch to playlists through Spotify for Artists and set up pre-saves.
  • Metadata matters: correct artist name, featured artists, songwriter credits, genre, and explicit tags. Fixing it later is painful.
  • Register as a songwriter with a performing rights organization (like BMI or ASCAP in the US) so you collect publishing royalties.
  • Also post on YouTube, SoundCloud, BandLab, and Bandcamp, where fans can find and support you directly.
  • Samples & beats: only release what you have the rights to. Clear samples or use CC0 and public-domain sounds.
B

Thumbnails & cover art

  • Readable at thumb size. Check it at 100 px wide. If you can't tell what it is, simplify.
  • One focal point: a face, an object, or a word. Not all three.
  • High contrast and 3 words of text or fewer. Big, bold, clean fonts.
  • Sizes: YouTube thumbnails 1280×720 (16:9). Streaming cover art 3000×3000 square.
  • Build a look: same font, color, and layout on every release, so fans recognize you mid-scroll.
  • Free tools: Canva, Photopea, and GIMP.
C

Consistency

  • Pick a schedule you can keep, like one release a month or a short clip three times a week. Steady beats viral.
  • One song = ten posts: the hook, the making-of, the beat breakdown, a lyric card, a reaction, a live take.
  • Shorts & Reels: the first 2 seconds decide everything. Open with the best part.
  • Batch your content. Film a week's posts in one session.
  • Reply to every comment early on. Your first 100 fans build the next 1,000.
  • Read your analytics monthly, then double down on what works.

A simple release plan

  1. Week −4Song mastered. Upload to your distributor. Cover art done.
  2. Week −3Pitch to playlists. Start teasing 10-second clips.
  3. Week −1Pre-save link, behind-the-scenes posts, tell your people.
  4. Release dayPost everywhere, go live, and reply to everyone.
  5. Week +1 to +4Keep posting new angles on the same song. Most songs grow after release week.

Keys to Being a Musician

Own your craft.

Gear changes. Trends change. These don't.

  1. 01

    Your voice is the asset

    Anyone can download a plugin. Nobody can download your perspective. Protect it, sharpen it, and don't sand it down to sound like someone else.

  2. 02

    Finish things

    Ten finished songs teach more than a hundred perfect loops. Done is a skill. Practice it.

  3. 03

    Own your work

    Know who owns your masters and your publishing. Read before you sign anything. If someone wants your ownership, ask what you're really getting back.

  4. 04

    Limits are a sound

    A phone mic and a free DAW have made real records. Constraints force decisions, and decisions become style.

  5. 05

    Consistency over hype

    One viral moment fades. Showing up every week compounds. Play the long game.

  6. 06

    Learn the why

    Copy your heroes to learn, then ask why it works: the tempo, the space, the chord. Understanding is what lets you break the rules on purpose.

  7. 07

    Community, not competition

    The artist next to you isn't your rival. Collaborate, share what you learn, lift people up. Scenes grow together.

  8. 08

    Protect the joy

    Numbers will lie to you some weeks. Make the music you'd play even if nobody pressed play. That's the part that lasts.
